AI Bat Fit Quiz — Website Leads

One campaign, one ad set, three reels, ₹1,000 a day. Day one worked. Day two did not, and the reason is now known.

9 Sep spent ₹464.92 and returned one lead. The campaign was paused at 7:04 PM on 8 Sep and switched back on at 7:17 AM on 9 Sep. That restart reset the learning phase, and within about 43 minutes Meta spent ₹463.50 — 99.7% of the day — on the cheapest inventory it could find. The quiz and the website were checked end to end and are working correctly.

The website is not the problem

Checked live, and confirmed in the pixel data

The obvious suspicion on a day like this is that the quiz broke. It did not. The store's own event data separates the two possibilities cleanly, because it records people starting the quiz as well as finishing it.

Reading the numbers. "Leads" are the campaign's optimisation event, offsite_conversion.fb_pixel_lead, on Meta's default attribution window. Store-side event counts are slightly higher than Meta's attributed leads because they include visitors who did not arrive from an ad. Placement, age and hour figures are separate breakdown queries and each sums to within ₹1 of the campaign total; small gaps are Meta's rounding, not missing spend. Cost per lead is spend ÷ leads within each row, so rows with no leads show no cost per lead rather than a zero.

Caveats. Two days, one ad set, still inside the learning phase. Every split below the campaign level is a small sample, and the 55+, Stories and women's rows in particular are too thin to act on alone. Day two produced too few leads to break down by audience at all. Region-level lead counts were withheld by Meta's privacy thresholds and are not shown.
